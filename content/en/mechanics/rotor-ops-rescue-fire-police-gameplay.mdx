## Tactical Procedures for Landing Zone (LZ) Operations

The highest percentage of rotary losses occur within 100 feet of the ground. Navigating active combat environments demands strict adherence to energy management, approach vectors, and dust-out recovery.

### Step-by-Step Tactical Extraction Protocol

Executing clean insertions or emergency evacuations requires following a strict procedural checklist:

1. **Zone Reconnaissance:** Before committing to an approach, conduct a wide orbit beyond light anti-aircraft artillery (AAA) range. Identify wind direction, power lines, and terrain-masking corridors.
2. **Terrain-Masked Ingress:** Drop down to nap-of-the-earth (NOE) altitude (below 50 feet AGL). Use tree lines, dry riverbeds, and urban structures to break the line of sight of hostile radar networks.
3. **Deceleration without Settling:** Transition through Effective Translational Lift (ETL) smoothly. Avoid descending faster than 300 feet per minute when ground speed falls below 30 knots to prevent entering dangerous Vortex Ring State (VRS).
4. **Touchdown and Perimeter Security:** Keep aircraft wheels or skids lightly on the deck without fully bottoming collective pitch if the soil is soft. Allow door gunners or AI squad elements to establish a 360-degree security ring.
5. **Masked Egress:** Lift vertically into ground effect, drop the nose to regain translational lift immediately, and depart along your planned exit corridor below tree level.

The checklist below outlines essential pre-flight and in-flight parameters for high-threat operations:

| Phase | Flight Parameter | Pilot Action | Hazard Mitigated |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ingress** | < 50 ft AGL / > 100 kts | Trim aircraft, cross-check radar altimeter | Radar detection, long-range SAMs |
| **Flare / Transition** | 40 kts down to 10 kts | Apply aft cyclic, balance with left/right pedal | Over-torquing engine, Vortex Ring State |
| **Hover / Landing** | Zero drift, < 200 fpm descent | Align nose into headwind, stabilize collective | Dynamic rollover, tail rotor strike |
| **Ground Operations** | Ground idle or flat pitch | Maintain cyclic center, monitor torque gauge | Unintended takeoff, rotor overspeed |
| **Egress** | Accelerate within ground effect | Push cyclic forward, maintain altitude < 15 ft | Exposing profile to enemy fire |

### Integrating Custom Scenario Markers

Modern mission engines read direct script tags placed inside templates. By understanding these logic hooks, creators can adapt standard military maps into specialized municipal or tactical rescue operations.

*   **IMPORT Markers:** Place these static placeholders on airbases, highway interchanges, or hospital helipads. During mission launch, the engine reads these coordinates and automatically builds fully furnished forward bases, field hospitals, or security barricades.
*   **Splash Damage Triggers:** High-explosive ordinance and rocket blasts simulate pressure waves. Indirect hits suppress infantry and damage soft-skinned light trucks, rewarding accurate suppressive fire rather than requiring pixel-perfect impacts.
*   **CTLD Logic Zones:** Set up supply depots where transport choppers can spawn rescue personnel, barricade materials, and light scout vehicles directly from the in-game communications menu.

When running heavily populated dynamic mission files, you might encounter unexpected AI pathing or performance issues. Review this diagnostic matrix to resolve common errors:

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What makes dynamic helicopter missions different from standard combat sorties?
Unlike pre-scripted missions with fixed triggers, dynamic rotary frameworks utilize dynamic frontline AI, procedural voiceovers, and integrated logistics. Objectives dynamically adapt based on battlefield changes, requiring players to switch between CAS, troop transport, CSAR, and recon on the fly.

### How do you avoid Vortex Ring State (VRS) during emergency steep approaches?
Vortex Ring State occurs when a helicopter settles into its own turbulent downwash—typically when forward airspeed drops below 30 knots and descent rates exceed 300 feet per minute. To avoid it, fly shallow approaches, keep your descent rate low, or use forward airspeed until settling into ground effect. If caught in VRS, lower collective pitch and push the cyclic forward or sideways (the Vuichard recovery technique) to fly out of the dirty air.

### Can dynamic rotary missions be played in cooperative multiplayer?
Yes. Mission generators create slots for both single-player sorties and multi-crew cooperative lobbies. Squadrons can assign individual pilots to specialized roles—such as pair elements providing gunship escort while transport crews extract casualties or insert tactical police teams.

### What are the best controller setups for high-intensity rotor ops rescue fire police gameplay?
Precision helicopter flight demands high-resolution controls. A full-length cyclic flight stick without a centering spring (or with light dampening), independent rudder pedals with high-precision sensors, and an authentic collective lever offer the exact inputs needed for stable hovering in confined rescue spots.
