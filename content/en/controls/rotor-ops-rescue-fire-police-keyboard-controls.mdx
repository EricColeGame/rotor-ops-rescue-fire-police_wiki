## Universal Flight Dynamics: Rotorcraft Movement and Physics

Rotorcraft piloting departs radically from fixed-wing flight. Helicopters require continuous management of four primary forces: lift, weight, thrust, and drag. On a standard keyboard, these mechanics are governed by split inputs across your left and right hands, or coordinated through a combination of keys and mouse axis movements.

Understanding how each command impacts your airframe prevents the common "pendulum effect," where novice pilots overcorrect cyclic inputs and enter an unrecoverable stall.

| Action / Flight Axis | Primary Keyboard Key | Secondary Input / Mouse | In-Flight Behavioral Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Increase Collective (Throttle Up)** | `Left Shift` / `W` | N/A | Increases rotor blade pitch; climbs vertically |
| **Decrease Collective (Throttle Down)**| `Left Ctrl` / `S` | N/A | Decreases rotor blade pitch; initiates rapid descent |
| **Pitch Forward (Nose Down)** | `W` (or `Up Arrow`) | `Mouse Forward` | Lowers nose, converts vertical lift into forward airspeed |
| **Pitch Aft (Nose Up)** | `S` (or `Down Arrow`) | `Mouse Backward` | Raises nose, bleeds off forward speed, initiates hover flare |
| **Roll Port (Bank Left)** | `A` (or `Left Arrow`) | `Mouse Left` | Angles rotor disc left, initiating a banking turn or sidestep |
| **Roll Starboard (Bank Right)** | `D` (or `Right Arrow`) | `Mouse Right` | Angles rotor disc right, initiating a banking turn or sidestep |
| **Rudder / Anti-Torque Yaw Left** | `Q` | `Z` / Pedals | Counteracts torque; swings tail right and nose left |
| **Rudder / Anti-Torque Yaw Right** | `E` | `C` / Pedals | Swings tail left and nose right; aligns fuselage on approach |

### Critical Bind Separations
1. **Unbind Throttle from Utility Keys:** Never share `Left Shift` or `Left Ctrl` with winch or siren toggles. A sudden change in rotor RPM during a hoist mission will send your rescue subject into the ground.
2. **Isolate Bailout / Exit Commands:** Remap your vehicle exit key away from common interaction keys like `E` or `F`. In high-stress police pursuits, accidentally jumping out of your chopper at 1,000 feet is an instant mission failure.
3. **Use Mouse Deadzones:** If you fly cyclic via mouse aim, set a small center deadzone. This ensures minor mouse bumps while adjusting keyboard buttons won't knock your aircraft out of a stable hover.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I stop my helicopter from spinning uncontrollably?
Uncontrolled yaw spinning occurs when anti-torque pedals are neglected. Helicopters have natural rotor torque that forces the fuselage to spin in the opposite direction of the rotor blades. Counter this immediately by pressing your anti-torque pedal keys (`Q` for left, `E` for right) until the nose stabilizes. Ensure you are not applying sudden collective spikes without compensating rudder input.

### Can I rebind rotor ops rescue fire police keyboard controls to a flight stick or HOTAS?
Yes, most flight simulators and tactical emergency games fully support DirectInput and XInput peripherals. You can map your cyclic and collective to physical joysticks and throttles while preserving keyboard hotkeys for complex secondary commands like sirens, thermal optics, and water drop valves.

### Why does my water bucket drop retardant prematurely?
This is typically caused by shared keybinds. If your water drop key is mapped to `Spacebar` or `Left Shift`, applying collective or triggering a siren can accidentally dump your tank. Remap emergency drops to dedicated single-purpose keys such as `B` or an auxiliary mouse thumb button.

### What is the best camera view for precision rescue hoist pickups?
While first-person cockpit view offers maximum immersion and instrument telemetry, third-person chase camera (toggled with `V` or mouse scroll) provides the spatial clearance needed to spot tree branches, power lines, and ground obstacles while keeping the hoist line directly centered over the target.
